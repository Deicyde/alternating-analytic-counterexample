\title[Alternating precomposition is not analytic in characteristic 2]{Precomposition on continuous alternating maps\\ is not analytic in characteristic 2}
\author{Jack McCarthy}

\begin{abstract}
For normed spaces $E,E',F$ over a nontrivially normed field $\kk$ and a finite set $\iota$, precomposition
$f\mapsto(m\mapsto m\circ(f,\dots,f))$ sends continuous linear maps $E\to E'$ to continuous linear maps between spaces of
continuous alternating maps. It is $C^n$ for every $n\in\N\cup\{\infty\}$ in every characteristic~\cite{PR}, and analytic
when $\abs{\iota}!$ is invertible in $\kk$ (by polarisation). We show that it is \emph{not} analytic in general: over every
complete ultrametric field of characteristic~$2$ that is not spherically complete, with $E=E'=\linf$ and
$\abs{\iota}=2$, analyticity fails at $0$. The proof is a finite parity identity for determinants in characteristic~$2$,
glued along $\linf$; the failure is witnessed by two coordinate projections of $\linf$. The result is formalized in Lean~4/Mathlib in the form ``the general
$C^\omega$ statement implies \texttt{False}''.
\end{abstract}
\maketitle

\section{Statement}
\subsection*{Notation}
For normed spaces, $\cL(E,E')$ is the space of continuous linear maps with the operator norm, and $\Alt^\iota(E,F)$ is the
normed space of continuous alternating $\iota$-linear maps $E^\iota\to F$, i.e.\ those vanishing whenever two arguments
coincide; we write $\Alt^2$ when $\abs\iota=2$. For a polynomial $f$ and a monomial $\mu$, $\coeff_\mu f$ is the coefficient of
$\mu$ in $f$. We write $c_0\subset\linf$ for the null sequences, $\1_S$ for the indicator of a set $S$, and $\1=\1_\N$.

Throughout, $\kk$ is a field which is complete for a nontrivial absolute value $\abs{\,\cdot\,}$ satisfying the
\emph{ultrametric inequality} $\abs{x+y}\le\max(\abs x,\abs y)$; we call such a field \emph{ultrametric}. (Absolute values
with this property are also called non-archimedean; this is a condition on the absolute value and is unrelated to
non-Archimedean \emph{ordered} fields.) The sup norms used below inherit the inequality. We assume that
\begin{itemize}
\item[(K1)] $\operatorname{char}\kk=2$;
\item[(K2)] $\kk$ is not spherically complete: there is a sequence of closed balls $B(c_n,r_n)$ such that
$\abs{c_{n+1}-c_n}\le r_n$, $r_0>r_1>\cdots$ and $\bigcap_n B(c_n,r_n)=\emptyset$.
\end{itemize}
The balls in (K2) are balls in $\kk$, and they are nested by the ultrametric inequality: for $a\in B(c_{n+1},r_{n+1})$ we have
$\abs{a-c_n}\le\max(\abs{a-c_{n+1}},\abs{c_{n+1}-c_n})\le\max(r_{n+1},r_n)=r_n$. In characteristic $2$ that inequality is in fact automatic: an absolute value satisfies it as soon as it
is bounded on the multiples of $1$, and here these are only $0$ and $1$. Such fields exist; an explicit one is given in
\S\ref{sec:field}. Let $E=\linf=\linf(\N,\kk)$ with the sup norm, a Banach
algebra under pointwise multiplication; write $m_a\in\cL(E,E)$ for multiplication by $a\in E$, so that
$\norm{m_a}=\norm a$ (evaluate at $\1$), and $e_i$ for the unit vectors. For $x,y\in E$ let
\[
W(x,y):=x\otimes y+y\otimes x\in\linf(\N\times\N,\kk),\qquad W(x,y)_{pq}=x_py_q+x_qy_p ,
\]
and let $F\subseteq\linf(\N\times\N,\kk)$ be the closed linear span of all $W(x,y)$. Every element of $F$ is a symmetric
matrix with zero diagonal (as $W(x,y)_{pp}=2x_py_p=0$) and a uniform limit of matrices of finite rank (as $W(x,y)$ has
rank $\le2$). By the ultrametric inequality $\norm{W(x,y)}\le\norm x\norm y$, and $W(x,x)=2\,x\otimes x=0$; so
$W\colon E\times E\to F$ is a bounded alternating bilinear map of norm $\le1$. Let
\[
Q\colon \cL(E,E)\to\cL\bigl(\Alt^2(E,F),\Alt^2(E,F)\bigr),\qquad Q(f)(m)=m\circ(f,f).
\]

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
$Q$ is not analytic at $0$. In particular $Q$ is not $C^\omega$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:false}
The statement ``for all nontrivially normed fields $\kk$, all normed spaces $E,E',F$ over $\kk$ and all finite $\iota$,
precomposition $\cL(E,E')\to\cL(\Alt^\iota(E',F),\Alt^\iota(E,F))$ is $C^\omega$'' is false. So is the weaker statement in which
$\kk$ ranges only over complete ultrametric fields of characteristic $2$, $E=E'$ and $F$ range only over Banach spaces, and
$\abs\iota=2$.
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:false} follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:main} and the existence of a field with (K1) and (K2). The rest of
this note proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main} in four steps.

\section{Step 1: analyticity forces a bilinear lift}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:lift}
Let $Q\colon X\to Z$ be a map between normed spaces with $Q(tf)=t^2Q(f)$. If $Q$ is analytic at $0$ then there is a bounded
bilinear $P\colon X\times X\to Z$ with $P(f,f)=Q(f)$ for all $f$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $\sum_n p_n$ be a power series of $Q$ at $0$. For fixed $f$ the one-variable power series
$\sum_n t^n\,p_n(f,\dots,f)$ and the polynomial $t^2Q(f)$ agree for small $t$. Power series coefficients in one variable
are unique over any nontrivially normed field, so $p_2(f,f)=Q(f)$. Take $P=p_2$. (No division by $2$ occurs, and $P$
need not be symmetric.)
\end{proof}

So assume that a bounded bilinear $P$ with $P(f,f)=Q(f)$ exists, and put, for $a,b,x,y\in E$,
\[
\Phi(a,b;x,y):=P(m_a,m_b)(W)(x,y)\in F .
\]
Then $\Phi$ is $4$-linear, and since $\norm{m_a}=\norm a$ and $\norm W\le1$ we have
\[
\norm{\Phi(a,b;x,y)}\le C\norm a\,\norm b\,\norm x\,\norm y,\qquad C:=\max(1,\norm P).
\]
Moreover
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Phi}
\Phi(a,b;x,x)=0,\qquad \Phi(a,a;x,y)=W(ax,ay).
\end{equation}
Since $2=0$, the first identity gives the symmetry $\Phi(a,b;x,y)=\Phi(a,b;y,x)$. We will contradict the existence of such
a $\Phi$.

\section{Step 2: a finite parity identity}
Fix a finite block $I\subset\N$ of even size $2m$ and $a',b'\in E$ with $\norm{a'},\norm{b'}\le1$ (in Step~4 they
will carry the earlier blocks). Let
$X_i,Y_i$ ($i\in I$) be indeterminates. Expanding $\Phi$ multilinearly in $x=\sum_{i\in I}X_ie_i$ and $y=\sum_{i\in I}Y_ie_i$
defines polynomials
\[
G_{pq}:=\Phi(a'+x,\,b'+y;\,a'+x,\,b'+y)_{pq}\in\kk[X,Y]\qquad(p,q\in I),
\]
whose values at points $x,y\in\kk^I$ (vectors supported on $I$) are the entries of an $I\times I$ matrix $G(x,y)$. The matrix
$G$ is symmetric with zero diagonal, each $G_{pq}$ has degree $\le2$ in $X$ and $\le2$ in $Y$, its part of bidegree $(2,2)$
is $g_{pq}:=\Phi(x,y;x,y)_{pq}$, and $G_{pq}-g_{pq}$ has total degree $\le3$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:g}
For $p\neq q$ we have, in $\kk[X,Y]$,
\[
g_{pq}(Y,X)=g_{pq}(X,Y)+X_p^2Y_q^2+X_q^2Y_p^2\qquad\text{and}\qquad g_{pq}(X,X)=0 .
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A field with a nontrivial absolute value is infinite, so it suffices to check these at points $x,y\in\kk^I$. By the symmetry in the last two slots,
$g(y,x)=\Phi(y,x;y,x)=\Phi(y,x;x,y)$, so by bilinearity in the first two slots and \eqref{eq:Phi}
\begin{align*}
g(x,y)+g(y,x)&=\Phi(x+y,x+y;x,y)+\Phi(x,x;x,y)+\Phi(y,y;x,y)\\
&=W\bigl((x+y)x,(x+y)y\bigr)+W(x^2,xy)+W(xy,y^2).
\end{align*}
Expanding, $W((x+y)x,(x+y)y)=W(x^2,xy)+W(x^2,y^2)+W(xy,xy)+W(xy,y^2)$. The first and last terms cancel against the two
other summands and $W(xy,xy)=0$, which leaves $W(x^2,y^2)$, whose $(p,q)$ entry is $x_p^2y_q^2+x_q^2y_p^2$. Finally
$g(x,x)=W(x^2,x^2)=0$.
\end{proof}

Let $\tau$ be the automorphism of $\kk[X,Y]$ swapping $X_i\leftrightarrow Y_i$, and $\delta$ the substitution $Y_i:=X_i$.

\begin{lemma}[balanced coefficients]\label{lem:bal}
Let $f\in\kk[X,Y]$ with $\tau f=f$ and $\operatorname{char}\kk=2$. For every $S\subseteq I$ the coefficient of
$\prod_{i\in S}X_iY_i$ in $f$ equals the coefficient of $\prod_{i\in S}X_i^2$ in $\delta f$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The coefficient of $\prod_{S}X_i^2$ in $\delta f$ is the sum of the coefficients in $f$ of all monomials $X^\alpha Y^\beta$ with
$\alpha+\beta=2\cdot\1_S$. The involution $(\alpha,\beta)\mapsto(\beta,\alpha)$ of this set of monomials preserves coefficients (as
$\tau f=f$), and its only fixed point is $\alpha=\beta=\1_S$; all other terms cancel in pairs.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:matching}
Let $M$ be a perfect matching of $I$ and $\nu:=\prod_{i\in I}X_iY_i$. Then
\[\coeff_\nu\prod_{\{p,q\}\in M}G_{pq}=1 .\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The monomial $\nu$ has total degree $4m$, the maximum possible, so only the top parts contribute:
\[\coeff_\nu\prod_{M} G_{pq}=\coeff_\nu\prod_{M} g_{pq}.\]
Put $g'_{pq}:=g_{pq}+X_p^2Y_q^2$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:g},
\[\tau g'_{pq}=g'_{pq}\qquad\text{and}\qquad \delta g'_{pq}=X_p^2X_q^2 .\]
A product containing a factor $X_p^2Y_q^2$ has no squarefree monomial, so replacing every $g_{pq}$ by $g'_{pq}$ does
not change the coefficient of $\nu$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:bal}, applied to the $\tau$-invariant product $\prod_M g'_{pq}$,
this coefficient is the coefficient of $\prod_{i\in I}X_i^2$ in
\[\delta\prod_{M} g'_{pq}=\prod_{\{p,q\}\in M}X_p^2X_q^2=\prod_{i\in I}X_i^2,\]
namely $1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:det}
Let $A$ be a symmetric matrix with zero diagonal over a commutative ring of characteristic $2$, of even size $2m$. Then
$\det A=\sum_M\prod_{\{p,q\}\in M}A_{pq}^2$, the sum being over perfect matchings; and the number of perfect matchings,
$(2m-1)!!$, is odd.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
In $\det A=\sum_\sigma\prod_iA_{i\sigma(i)}$ (all signs are $1$), $\sigma$ and $\sigma^{-1}$ contribute equally by symmetry, so
only involutions survive; those with a fixed point contribute $0$; a fixed-point-free involution is a perfect matching
and contributes $\prod A_{pq}^2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[hypercube sum]\label{lem:cube}
Let $R$ be a commutative ring of characteristic $2$ and let $f\in R[Z_1,\dots,Z_N]$ have total degree $\le N$. Then
$\sum_{z\in\{0,1\}^N}f(z)$ is the coefficient of $Z_1Z_2\cdots Z_N$ in $f$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By linearity we may take $f$ to be a monomial $Z^\alpha$. At a point $z\in\{0,1\}^N$ it takes the value $1$ if $z_i=1$ for all
$i$ in the support of $\alpha$, and $0$ otherwise; so the sum is $2^{N-\abs{\operatorname{supp}\alpha}}$, which vanishes in $R$
unless every variable occurs in $Z^\alpha$. The only monomial of degree $\le N$ in which all $N$ variables occur is
$Z_1\cdots Z_N$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:point}
The values of $\det G$ at the $0/1$ points sum to $1$,
\[\sum_{x,y\in\{0,1\}^I}\det G(x,y)=1,\]
and consequently there are $x,y\in\{0,1\}^I$ with $\abs{\det G(x,y)}\ge1$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For a perfect matching $M$ the product $\prod_{\{p,q\}\in M}G_{pq}$ has total degree $\le4m$, which is the number of variables
$X_i,Y_i$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:cube} and Proposition~\ref{prop:matching} its sum over the $0/1$ points is therefore
$\coeff_\nu\prod_MG_{pq}=1$. Squaring is additive in characteristic $2$, so by Lemma~\ref{lem:det}
\[
\sum_{x,y\in\{0,1\}^I}\det G(x,y)=\sum_M\Bigl(\,\sum_{x,y\in\{0,1\}^I}\ \prod_{\{p,q\}\in M}G_{pq}(x,y)\Bigr)^2=(2m-1)!!\cdot1=1,
\]
because $(2m-1)!!$ is odd. If all the values $\det G(x,y)$ had absolute value $<1$, the ultrametric inequality would give
$\abs1<1$.
\end{proof}

The point is that the coefficient $1$ does not depend on the unknown lift $P$: all of its freedom is carried by
$\tau$-invariant terms vanishing on the diagonal $Y=X$, which Lemma~\ref{lem:bal} cannot see. No single pair $(x,y)$
works for every lift, but the $2^{4m}$ vertices of the cube cannot all fail at once.

\section{Step 3: locality on \texorpdfstring{$\linf$}{l-infinity}}
For $x\in\linf$ write $\tail Nx$ for $x$ with its coordinates below $N$ set to $0$.
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:nsc}
Under \textup{(K2)}, a bounded linear functional $\varphi$ on $\linf$ with $\varphi(e_i)=0$ for all $i$ is zero.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\varphi$ kills finitely supported sequences, so $\abs{\varphi(x)}\le\norm\varphi\sup_{i\ge M}\abs{x_i}$ for every $M$. Suppose
$\varphi\ne0$. For every $0<s<\norm\varphi$ there is $w$ with $\varphi(w)=1$ and $\norm w\le1/s$; fix one such vector $w_0$. Because
$r_{n+1}<r_n$ we can choose $s_n\in\linf$ with $\varphi(s_n)=c_{n+2}-c_{n+1}$ and $\norm{s_n}\le r_n/\norm\varphi$: take
$s_n=(c_{n+2}-c_{n+1})w$ with $\norm w\le r_n/(r_{n+1}\norm\varphi)$. Let $x_n:=c_1w_0+\sum_{k<n}s_k$; then $\varphi(x_n)=c_{n+1}$
and $\norm{x_{n+d}-x_n}\le r_n/\norm\varphi$ by the ultrametric inequality. The diagonal sequence $y_i:=x_i(i)$ is bounded, since
$\norm{x_i}\le\max(\norm{x_0},r_0/\norm\varphi)$, and $\abs{y_i-x_n(i)}\le r_n/\norm\varphi$ for $i\ge n$. Hence
$\abs{\varphi(y)-c_{n+1}}=\abs{\varphi(y-x_n)}\le r_n$, and since $\abs{c_{n+1}-c_n}\le r_n$ the ultrametric inequality gives
$\abs{\varphi(y)-c_n}\le r_n$ for every $n$. So $\varphi(y)\in\bigcap_nB(c_n,r_n)$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}
This is the direction of van der Put's theorem~\cite{vdP} that we need, $(\linf/c_0)'=0$; the index shift absorbs the usual
$(1+\varepsilon)$ loss, and no Hahn--Banach argument is used.

\begin{proposition}[multilinear Phillips property]\label{prop:tails}
Let $\lambda$ be a bounded $d$-linear form on $\linf$ and $\varepsilon>0$. There is $N$ such that
$\abs{\lambda(u_1,\dots,u_d)}\le\varepsilon$ whenever $\norm{u_j}\le1$ for all $j$ and $u_j=\tail N{u_j}$ for some $j$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Sketch \textup{(the complete proof is in the Lean development)}]
\emph{The case $d=1$}, $\lambda=\varphi$. (a) $\varphi(e_i)\to0$. Otherwise $\abs{\varphi(e_{i_k})}\ge\varepsilon$ along a subsequence. Let $T$ be
the bounded operator with $T(x)_{i_k}=x_k/\varphi(e_{i_k})$ and all other coordinates $0$, and $\psi:=\varphi\circ T$, so that
$\psi(e_k)=1$ for all $k$. With $\sh$ the right shift, $\psi\circ(1-\sh)$ kills every $e_k$, hence vanishes by
Proposition~\ref{prop:nsc}; but $(1-\sh)\1=e_0$, so $0=\psi(e_0)=1$. (b) For fixed $x$,
$\varphi(\tail Nx)-\varphi(\tail{N+1}x)=x_N\varphi(e_N)\to0$, so $\varphi(\tail Nx)$ converges; the limit is a bounded linear functional
of $x$ killing every $e_i$, hence it is $0$. (c) If the claim failed for $\varepsilon$, then (b) would give unit vectors $w_k$ with
disjoint finite supports moving to infinity and $\abs{\varphi(w_k)}>\varepsilon$; the functional
$c\mapsto\varphi(\sum_kc_kw_k)$ (coordinatewise sum) then contradicts (a).

\emph{The case $d>1$}, by induction on $d$. Suppose the claim fails for $\varepsilon$. First, for every $M$ there is a unit tuple
with $\abs\lambda>\varepsilon$ \emph{all} of whose slots vanish below $M$: start from a witness one of whose slots is supported very far
out, and split any other slot into its part below $M$, a finite combination of the $e_i$, and its tail; the inductive
hypothesis, applied to the finitely many $(d-1)$-linear forms obtained by freezing that slot at $e_i$, $i<M$, shows that
the part below $M$ contributes at most $\varepsilon$, so the tail carries $\abs\lambda>\varepsilon$. Next, by (b) each slot can be cut down to
a finite window. Finally choose such tuples $z^{(s)}$ with windows moving to infinity fast enough and let $x$ be the
coordinatewise sum of their first slots. Then $\lambda(x,z^{(s)}_2,\dots,z^{(s)}_d)$ equals $\lambda(z^{(s)})$, plus finitely many
terms with an earlier first slot (each $\le\varepsilon$ by the inductive hypothesis for $\lambda(z^{(r)}_1,\cdot)$, $r<s$), plus one
term with a far first slot ($\le\varepsilon$ by the case $d=1$). So it has absolute value $>\varepsilon$ for every $s$, contradicting the
inductive hypothesis for the $(d-1)$-linear form $\lambda(x,\cdot)$.
\end{proof}
This is exactly where $\linf$ and (K2) are used; the statement is false for $c_0$ and for spherically complete fields.

\section{Step 4: gluing}
Choose inductively blocks $I_1<I_2<\cdots$ with $\abs{I_t}=2t$ and vectors $a_t,b_t\in\{0,1\}^{I_t}$ as follows.
Given the earlier stages let $a'=\sum_{s<t}a_s$, $b'=\sum_{s<t}b_s$, and apply Corollary~\ref{cor:point} to get $a_t,b_t$ with
$\abs{\det G_t}\ge1$, where $G_t=\Phi(a'+a_t,b'+b_t;a'+a_t,b'+b_t)|_{I_t\times I_t}$. Put $\varepsilon_t:=\tfrac12C^{-2t}$ and use
Proposition~\ref{prop:tails} (with $d=4$) for the finitely many entry forms $\Phi(\cdot)_{pq}$, $p,q\in I_t$, to place all later
blocks so far out that these forms are at most $\varepsilon_t$ on unit arguments one of which is supported there.

Let $a,b\in\linf$ be defined by $a|_{I_t}=a_t$, $b|_{I_t}=b_t$ and zero elsewhere. These are not norm-convergent sums;
$a=\1_A$ and $b=\1_B$ are indicator functions of two subsets of $\N$, so $m_a,m_b$ are coordinate projections. Let
$\mathcal O:=\Phi(a,b;a,b)\in F$. Splitting $a=a^{\le t}+a^{>t}$, $b=b^{\le t}+b^{>t}$ and using multilinearity,
$\norm{\mathcal O|_{I_t\times I_t}-G_t}\le\varepsilon_t$. All entries are bounded by $C$. Expanding the determinant multilinearly in
columns, every term containing a column of the perturbation is at most $\varepsilon_tC^{2t-1}<1$, so the ultrametric inequality
gives
\[
\abs{\det\mathcal O|_{I_t\times I_t}}=\abs{\det G_t}\ge1\qquad\text{for every }t .
\]
On the other hand $\mathcal O\in F$ is within $\eta:=(2C)^{-1}$ of a matrix $R$ of some finite rank $r$. Expanding
$\det(R+(\mathcal O-R))$ on $I_t\times I_t$ in the same way, terms with more than $r$ columns from $R$ vanish, so
$\abs{\det\mathcal O|_{I_t\times I_t}}\le C^r\eta^{2t-r}<1$ once $t>r$. This contradiction proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. \qed

\section{An explicit field}\label{sec:field}
Let $k_0=\mathbb F_2$ (or any field of characteristic $2$). For
$j\ge0$ let $k_0((t^{1/2^j}))$ be the field of \emph{formal} Laurent series $\sum_{n\ge N}a_nt^{n/2^j}$ ($N\in\Z$, $a_n\in k_0$) in
the single variable $t^{1/2^j}$. These fields form an
increasing chain, and we let
\[
\cP:=\bigcup_{j\ge0}k_0((t^{1/2^j})),\qquad \abs f:=2^{-\operatorname{ord}f},
\]
be the field of \emph{$2$-power Puiseux series}. Here $\operatorname{ord}f$ is the minimum $q$ for which $\coeff_{t^q}f\ne0$. We set $\abs0=0$ by definition.
Let $\kk$ be the completion of $\cP$ for this (ultrametric) absolute value. This is characteristic $2$ since
$k_0\hookrightarrow\cP\hookrightarrow\kk$.

To verify (K2), put
\[
\gamma_m:=1-2^{-(m+1)},\qquad
c_n:=\sum_{m=0}^{n}t^{\gamma_m}\in\cP,\qquad r_n:=2^{-\gamma_{n+1}} .
\]
\emph{The balls have the form required in \textup{(K2)}.} Since $c_{n+1}-c_n=t^{\gamma_{n+1}}$ we have
$\abs{c_{n+1}-c_n}=2^{-\gamma_{n+1}}=r_n$. Since the $\gamma_m$ are strictly increasing to $1$, the $r_n$ are strictly decreasing, and $r_n>2^{-1}=\tfrac12$.

\emph{The intersection is empty.} Suppose that $a\in\kk$ satisfies $\abs{a-c_n}\le r_n$ for all $n$. As $\cP$ is dense in $\kk$
there is $y\in\cP$ with $\abs{a-y}<\tfrac12$, and then for every $n$
\[
\abs{y-c_n}\le\max\bigl(\abs{y-a},\abs{a-c_n}\bigr)\le\max\bigl(\tfrac12,r_n\bigr)=r_n .
\]
Now $y\in k_0((t^{1/2^j}))$ for some $j$. The exponent
$\gamma_j=(2^{j+1}-1)/2^{j+1}$ has an odd numerator, so $\gamma_j\notin2^{-j}\Z$ and $\coeff_{t^{\gamma_j}}y=0$. For $n\ge j$ the
term $t^{\gamma_j}$ occurs in $c_n$, so
\[
\coeff_{t^{\gamma_j}}(y-c_n)=0-1\ne0,\qquad\text{hence}\qquad \operatorname{ord}(y-c_n)\le\gamma_j
\quad\text{and}\quad \abs{y-c_n}\ge2^{-\gamma_j}.
\]
But $\gamma_j<\gamma_{n+1}$ because $j<n+1$, so $2^{-\gamma_j}>2^{-\gamma_{n+1}}=r_n$. Thus $\abs{y-c_n}>r_n$, contradicting the previous
display.

Informally, the only candidate for a common point of the balls is the formal sum $\sum_mt^{\gamma_m}$. Its exponents have
unbounded denominators and all lie below $1$, so its terms do not tend to $0$ and the partial sums $c_n$ are not a
Cauchy sequence; the sum exists in the (spherically complete) Hahn series field $k_0((t^{\mathbb Q}))$ but not in $\kk$.
Completing $\cP$ does add series with unbounded denominators, such as $\sum_mt^{m+2^{-m}}$, but only with exponents
tending to $\infty$: below any bound, the exponents of an element of $\kk$ still have a common denominator. (The
completion of an algebraic closure of $\mathbb F_2((t))$ is another example of a field with (K1) and (K2).)

\section{Remarks}
\begin{remark}[Why characteristic $2$, why $\linf$, why this $F$]
In characteristic $\neq2$ the lift $\tfrac12\bigl(m\circ(f,g)+m\circ(g,f)\bigr)$ exists, and the parity argument breaks
(pairs add instead of cancelling). If $E$ has an orthogonal base $(e_i)$, e.g.\ $E=c_0$, and $F$ is Banach, then
\[
P(f,g)(m)(x,y)=\sum_{i<j}(x_iy_j-x_jy_i)\,m(fe_i,ge_j)
\]
is a bounded lift in every characteristic, so $Q$ is analytic; a similar formula works for a base of $E'$, and
$t$-orthogonal bases suffice. This covers finite-dimensional $E$ and all Banach spaces over a discretely valued
$\kk$~\cite{vR}, so the counterexample needs a dense valuation; one can also show that $Q$ is analytic when $F$ is
spherically complete. On $c_0$ the glued vectors $a,b$ of Step~4 do not exist and Proposition~\ref{prop:tails} fails. For
$E=\linf$ and the larger target $\linf(\N\times\N,\kk)\supseteq F$ a bounded lift also exists (sort the indices entrywise),
but it sends $W$ to matrices such as the ``staircase'' $\1_{\{(p,q)\,:\,p\text{ odd},\,q\text{ even},\,p<q\}}$, which are
not limits of finite-rank matrices, so it leaves $F$. These positive statements are not proved here and are not part of
the formalization.
\end{remark}
\begin{remark}[What is forced]
No single pair $(x,y)$ works for every lift: adding to $\Phi$ a bounded $4$-linear map with values in $F$ that is
alternating in $(a,b)$ and in $(x,y)$ gives another admissible $\Phi$, and this can change a given off-diagonal entry
of $G(x,y)$ by an arbitrary scalar. Blocks of bounded size cannot give a contradiction either: for $u=\1_{\mathrm{odd}}$, $v=\1_{\mathrm{even}}$ the
rank-two matrix $W(u,v)\in F$ has the entry $1$ in every diagonal block $\{2s-1,2s\}$. On a finite block the sorted lift
of the previous remark has norm $1$ and satisfies all the identities of Step~2, so there is no finite-dimensional
obstruction: the invariant is the rank of one element of $F$ against the size of the blocks, and Step~3 is what allows
infinitely many blocks to be glued into one element.
\end{remark}
\begin{remark}[Higher degree, other characteristics]
On paper, in characteristic $2$ every $\abs\iota\ge2$ is covered by wedging with a coordinate of $E\oplus\kk$; this is not
formalized. In odd characteristic $\ell$, for forms of degree $\ell$, the question is open: odd-degree forms have no
Pfaffian-type top power, so a different invariant is needed.
\end{remark}

\section{Formalization}\label{sec:lean}
The Lean~4/Mathlib development is at
\begin{center}\url{https://github.com/Deicyde/alternating-analytic-counterexample}\end{center}
The library \texttt{AltCounter/} contains no \texttt{sorry}, and the only axioms it uses are the three standard ones
(\texttt{propext}, \texttt{Quot.sound} and \texttt{Classical.choice}). Its headline theorem assumes that Mathlib's
\begin{center}\texttt{ContinuousAlternatingMap.compContinuousLinearMapCLM}\end{center}
is $C^\omega$ for all nontrivially normed fields, normed spaces and finite index types, and derives \texttt{False}; a second
theorem does the same from the weaker statement in Theorem~\ref{thm:false}. Both are restated in a Mathlib-only file
\texttt{Challenge.lean} (with \texttt{sorry} in place of the proofs, as that format requires) and checked against
\texttt{Solution.lean} by the \texttt{leanprover/comparator} tool. Steps~1--4 and \S\ref{sec:field} are formalized, including the complete proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:tails} by
induction on $d$; Lemma~\ref{lem:cube} is \texttt{sum\_eval\_cubePt}, and the field of \S\ref{sec:field} is built with
$k_0=\mathbb F_2$. The Lean constants in Step~4 are chosen with more slack than here, and the Lean form of
Corollary~\ref{cor:point} records only that the witnesses lie in the unit ball, which is all that Step~4 uses. Not formalized: non-analyticity at base points other than $0$, index types with more than two
elements, the positive statements in the remarks, and the bundle-level consequence (no $C^\omega$ vector-bundle structure on
bundles of continuous alternating maps in this generality).
