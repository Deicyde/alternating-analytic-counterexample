## The question

The note calls a **bilinear lift** of a map `Q : X → Z` that is homogeneous of degree 2 (with bilinear polar form
`Q(f+g) − Q(f) − Q(g)`) a bounded bilinear map `P : X × X → Z`, not necessarily symmetric, with `P(f,f) = Q(f)`. In the note
`X = L(E,E)`, `Q(f)(m) = m∘(f,f)` on continuous alternating 2-forms, and the main result is that over a complete ultrametric
field of characteristic 2 which is not spherically complete there are `E`, `F` for which no bounded bilinear lift exists.
Question: is there pre-existing literature on such lifts?

## Summary of findings

**The notion is classical in algebra, under several names.**
- A quadratic form `Q` on a module is `β(x,x)` for a bilinear `β` that need not be symmetric; two such `β` differ by an
  alternating form; on a finitely generated projective module such a `β` always exists, and for a free module the proof is
  the "sorted lift" used in the note (Schulze-Pillot, Lemma 1.12; Elman–Karpenko–Merkurjev §7, the exact sequence
  `0 → Alt → Bil → Quad → 0` over a field).
- Names found: "bilinear lift" (Scully 2024, for symmetric `b` over a field of characteristic 2, where existence is automatic
  and non-uniqueness is the question); "trace of a bilinear form" (Eilenberg–Mac Lane, Galindo); "expansion"
  (Izhakian–Knebusch–Rowen); "associated quadratic form" (Elman–Karpenko–Merkurjev).

**Algebraic results in which a lift does NOT exist** (the closest analogues of the note's theorem).
- Galindo 2025: for abelian groups every quadratic form lifts if and only if the group is 2-torsion free; `q : Z/2 → Z/4` with
  `q(1)` of order 4 has no lift. This answers a question posed as open by Braunling (2021).
- Garibaldi–Nakano 2016: in characteristic 2, invariant quadratic forms on irreducible modules of reductive groups with no
  invariant bilinear lift; the obstruction is a class in `H¹(G, Λ²V)`.

**The reformulation through symmetric tensors.** A degree-2 polynomial law is a linear map on the divided power `Γ²(M)`
(Roby 1963); quadratic maps between modules have a universal object `P²_R(M)` (Gaudier–Hartl 2009); polynomial laws and the
universal divided power algebra are being formalized in Lean/Mathlib (Chambert-Loir–de Frutos-Fernández, CPP '26).
`Γⁿ` and the symmetric tensors `TSⁿ` can differ for non-flat modules (Lundkvist 2008).

**Analysis.** Over R and C polarisation makes every continuous homogeneous polynomial the diagonal of a symmetric
multilinear map, so the question does not arise (Bochnak–Siciak 1971, text not read; Floret on symmetric tensor products).
The nearest precedent for "smooth but not analytic in positive characteristic" is Glöckner (2005), by a different mechanism.
The engine of the note's counterexample — failure of Hahn–Banach over fields that are not spherically complete, `ℓ^∞/c₀` has
zero dual — is classical (Ingleton 1952, van der Put 1969, van Rooij 1978) and is stated cleanly in Datta–Murayama (§2.2).

**Not found anywhere by this search.**
- Bounded or continuous bilinear lifts between Banach or non-archimedean normed spaces.
- Analyticity (or its failure) of exterior-power / alternating-map functors in characteristic `p`.
- Complementation of the symmetric tensors in a completed tensor square over a non-spherically-complete field.
- A purely ring-theoretic counterexample (a module over a commutative ring with a quadratic form that is not `β(x,x)`);
  Schulze-Pillot remarks that such a counterexample seems hard to find.

## Quadratic forms versus bilinear forms (pure algebra)

**Terminology found under this angle.** "Q(x) = beta(x,x) for a not necessarily symmetric bilinear form beta" (the standard book phrasing: Schulze-Pillot Lemma 1.12, Knus-style ring theory); "associated quadratic form" phi_b of a bilinear form b (Elman-Karpenko-Merkurjev); "trace of a bilinear form" / trace map Tr : Bil -> Quad (Eilenberg-Mac Lane, Braunling, Galindo); "bilinear lift" (Scully 2024, for symmetric b over a char-2 field; the note's exact phrase, weaker notion); "diagonal" of a bilinear form; the exact sequence 0 -> Alt -> Bil -> Quad -> 0 (equivalently Quad = Bil/Alt); polar form / polarisation (the opposite direction); "quadratic refinement" (of a symplectic/alternating form) - the OTHER direction, often confused with this one; universal quadratic object: divided square Gamma^2_R(M), symmetric tensor square TS^2/Sym^2, P^2_R(M) (Roby, Gaudier-Hartl); in geometry/representation theory: "lifting a quadratic form to a bilinear form", obstruction in H^1(G, Lambda^2 V)


### Rainer Schulze-Pillot, "Lecture Notes on Quadratic Forms and their Arithmetic", 2021 (arXiv:2008.12847v2, math.NT)

- **Seen at:** <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.12847>
- **Location:** Ch. 1, Lemma 1.12 (a)-(d) and its proof, p. 4-5; Example 1.9(c), p. 4; Remark 7.21, p. ~ (Clifford algebra chapter)
- **What it proves:** For a quadratic form Q on an R-module M with values in S: (b) for any not-necessarily-symmetric bilinear beta, x -> beta(x,x) is quadratic with polar form beta + beta^t; (c) two bilinear forms inducing the same Q differ by an alternating form; (d) if M is finitely generated PROJECTIVE, every Q is beta(x,x) for some non-symmetric beta. The proof for free M is literally the sorted lift: beta(v_i,v_i)=Q(v_i), beta(v_i,v_j)=b(v_i,v_j) for i<j, 0 for i>j; the projective case is done by extending Q to a free module and restricting beta. Example 1.9(c) shows over F_2 that a SYMMETRIC beta need not exist (Q(x)=x_1x_2). Remark 7.21 says Clifford-algebra injectivity only needs Q(v)=beta(v,v), and that a counterexample to that condition "seems to be hard to find".
- **Relation to bilinear lifts:** Same notion, verbatim, in pure algebra: this is exactly the note's bilinear lift (P not required symmetric), its sorted-lift construction, and its uniqueness-up-to-alternating statement. Positive results only; no norms/boundedness, and no counterexample.

### Cesar Galindo, "A Note on the Eilenberg-Mac Lane Isomorphism for Quadratic Forms", 2025 (arXiv:2507.15726v2)

- **Seen at:** <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.15726>
- **Location:** Section 5.1: exact sequence (5.1), Lemma 5.1, Theorem 5.3, Proposition 5.4, Corollary 5.5, Remark 5.6
- **What it proves:** For abelian groups G, M, calls q(x)=c(x,x) the TRACE of a bilinear form c, and has the exact sequence 0 -> Hom(Lambda^2 G, M) -> Hom(G tensor G, M) -> Quad(G,M) -> H^3_{2-ab}(G,M) -> H^3(G,M). Lemma 5.1: exactness at Quad (i.e. every quadratic form lifts) is equivalent to vanishing of the forgetful map s. Theorem 5.3: an individual q is a trace iff |x| q(x)=0 for every x in the 2-primary torsion T_2(G). Corollary 5.5: every q in Quad(G,M) lifts, for every M, iff G is 2-torsion free. It also gives a complete criterion and identifies the obstruction cohomologically. The mechanism is 2-torsion, not the note's completeness/boundedness failure, so it is an analogue, not the same theorem.
